# Prep Document Structure

Framework for building interaction-specific preparation documents.

## Document Sections

Every prep doc follows this structure. Sections are required unless marked optional.

### 1. The Backstory
- How the interaction originated
- What the contact already knows about the user
- What this means for the user's position (cold applicant vs validated peer vs referred candidate)

### 2. Know Your Position
- Table summarizing leverage factors
- Mindset framing for the interaction

### 3. Known Concerns (if any)
- Concerns or gaps already surfaced in prior communication
- For each: what was said, why it matters less than it seems (if applicable), and a concrete framing strategy
- Exact language the user can use if the concern comes up again

### 4. Opening the Conversation
- Specific opening strategy tailored to the interaction type and backstory
- 2-3 concrete opener lines the user can use
- How to set the right tone for the conversation

### 5. Arsenal: Stories Ready to Tell
- 3-4 top stories from the user's arsenal, selected for this specific interaction
- For each story:
  - **What it is**: One-liner summary
  - **Why it matters for this role**: Connection to the role and company
  - **Tradeoff to discuss**: A technical or strategic decision worth highlighting
  - **Company value alignment**: Which value(s) this demonstrates (without name-dropping)
- Ordered by relevance to the interaction

### 6. Questions to Ask
- Ordered by priority
- Grouped by theme (Understanding the Role, Understanding the Team, Understanding the Fit)
- Each question includes why it matters and what the answer tells the user
- A "natural closer" question that ties back to the backstory
- Tailored to the contact's role -- ask a Staff SWE different questions than a recruiter or VP

### 7. Company Values -- How Your Work Maps
- Table or section mapping each company value to specific evidence from the user's work
- Framed as "let your answers naturally align" -- not name-dropping

### 8. Things to Avoid
- Interaction-specific anti-patterns
- Common mistakes for this interaction type
- Specific things NOT to do based on the backstory

### 9. Logistics
- When, where, format, who, expected duration
- Any preparation logistics (calendar invite, video link, etc.)

### 10. After the Interaction
- Follow-up strategy (where to send thank-you, what medium)
- What to note and log
- Pointer to `/thing-i-did` for arsenal capture

## Tone Calibration by Interaction Type

### Coffee Chat
- Peer-to-peer framing throughout
- Emphasize mutual evaluation and shared interests
- Stories should feel like natural conversation, not rehearsed pitches
- Questions should feel curious, not interrogative
- "Things to Avoid" should include: don't treat it like a formal interview

### Phone Screen
- Professional but warm
- Emphasize motivation and fit
- Stories should be concise (2-minute versions)
- Include an elevator pitch for "tell me about yourself"
- Questions should show you've researched the company

### Technical
- Technical depth in story descriptions
- Include specific technologies, numbers, and architecture details
- Tradeoff discussions should be detailed
- Questions should probe technical culture and decision-making

### Onsite
- Full behavioral depth
- Multiple stories per value area
- Anti-pattern awareness from company profile
- Include stage-by-stage breakdown if multiple rounds

### Bar Raiser
- Cross-functional emphasis
- Judgment and ambiguity handling
- Long-term thinking and organizational impact
- Expect probing follow-ups -- prep second and third layers of each story
